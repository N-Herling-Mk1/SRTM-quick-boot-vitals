GLOSSARY

what the numbers on this page actually mean

What this page is for

A value on a screen carries no evidence of its own freshness. A frozen board and a healthy one both render as numbers. Everything here exists to tell those two apart while you develop against the WinCC panels.

Every query is a read against Prof. Cheu's production TIG stack (Telegraf → InfluxDB → Grafana). Nothing is written, no container restarted, no config edited.

Cadence map — the coloured grid

One cell per channel. Colour encodes how often that channel changes. It is a taxonomy, not an alarm: red means "rarely changes", not "broken". A serial number that never changes is red and that is correct.

  • green — median gap ≤ 30s
  • yellow — 30s to 5 minutes
  • orange — 5 minutes to 24 hours
  • red — over 24 hours, including channels that never changed during measurement
  • grey — not measured; run tools/collect_cadence.py

The edges were cut from the measured distribution, not chosen in advance. Both fall in empty regions — nothing sits near a boundary, so a small change in cadence cannot flip a colour.

Nothing on the map blinks or pulses. These colours describe what a channel is, not that anything needs attention.

Each channel card below carries the same colour as a thin outline, so the grid and the map agree at a glance.

The 5-second floor

Telegraf polls every 5 seconds. That is the fastest change this data can express: a rail jittering every 200ms and one changing every 4.9s are indistinguishable here. Roughly a third of all channels sit at exactly 5.0s for that reason. Finer resolution would need subscription_interval on Telegraf's OPC UA input.

t_poll and the header lamp

Seconds since Telegraf last wrote anything. Catches a dead pipeline — container stopped, VPN dropped, collector crashed. Blind to a frozen board: Telegraf writes on schedule whether or not the value moved.

The lamp top-right reports this and nothing else. It makes one claim — the collector is or is not writing — and deliberately makes no judgement about the board itself.

t_change (tΔ)

Seconds since a channel's value last actually differed. Derived from value transitions rather than timestamps, so it stays correct even though Telegraf polls rather than subscribes. Read it against that channel's cadence: 700s is alarming for a 5-second rail and unremarkable for a 2-hour counter.

t_wincc

Not built yet (mk_1). Would measure how long since the WinCC datapoint for the same channel changed. t_change small + t_wincc large = the panel is stale over live data — the bug this project exists to catch.

Canary counters

Monotonic counters only ever count upward, so one that stops advancing is unambiguously frozen. Most channels cannot do this job: a voltage rail may sit flat and be perfectly healthy.

Only the fast ones drive the t_change clock. They are not equally useful. Measurement showed FF11/12/13_uptime tick only every 7200s — exactly two hours — so they cannot detect a freeze for up to two hours. IPMC_seq, IPMC_rawtime and IPMC_time move on the order of seconds and are the ones worth watching for live liveness.

Channel classes

  • COUNTER — monotonic. Unambiguous when stopped.
  • ANALOG — continuous sensor. Flat is suspicious but not proof: many are quantised coarser than their real variation.
  • DISCRETE — flag or bitfield. Flat for hours is correct.
  • STATIC — identity string. Never changes by design.
  • UNKNOWN — in InfluxDB but not in the classification file, e.g. the retired SRTM_SPI* channels and Quality.

Detail view: value statistics

Computed over the window you select (30m to 7d).

  • current — most recent value
  • mean — average value; answers drift, not liveness
  • sigma — standard deviation of the value. A frozen channel has sigma exactly 0 — but so does a genuinely quiet rail, or one whose ADC step is coarser than its real noise. Shown, never alarmed.
  • min / max — observed range
  • t_change — as above

Detail view: change rhythm

Statistics of the interval between consecutive value changes, over the same window. Distinct from the value statistics above: this measures rhythm, those measure magnitude.

  • changes — number of transitions observed
  • mean gap — average seconds between changes
  • sigma gap — standard deviation of that interval. Near zero is a metronome; large means it changes in bursts.
  • median gap — the typical interval. More robust than the mean, which one long stall can drag upward by orders of magnitude.
  • min / max gap — shortest and longest observed interval

Reference cadence (24h)

From data/cadence_stats.csv, measured once over a full day by tools/collect_cadence.py. The window statistics above can be compared against it: a channel whose current rhythm has drifted from its 24-hour reference is doing something new.

  • p10 / p90 — 10th and 90th percentile gap. The spread between them says whether the channel has a rhythm at all.
  • burstiness — p90 divided by median. 1.0 is a metronome; 50× means it normally changes fast and then stalls for long stretches. Two channels can share a median of 5s and behave nothing alike.
  • n gaps — how many intervals the reference is based on. Small numbers mean a weak measurement.
